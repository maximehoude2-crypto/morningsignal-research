Executive Summary
A high-level read on the week’s dominant catalysts and positioning implications.

The four captured sessions were defined by a violent repricing of the AI complex rather than a steady directional trend. Semiconductors and crowded momentum sold off on July 28, the combination of an oil spike and a hawkish rate response deepened the drawdown on July 29, and Microsoft's earnings reversed the move on July 30 by providing evidence that AI infrastructure spending can convert into cloud profit. The week ended with Amazon up 15.32% and Apple down 7.35%, reinforcing that the market was rewarding company-specific earnings conversion rather than buying mega-cap technology as one basket.

SPY gained 1.08% and QQQ 0.86% across the available July 28-31 observations, while IWM lost 0.59%. Consumer Discretionary led sectors at +4.74%, driven heavily by Amazon, and Energy gained 2.05% as crude and Iran-related risk supported cash-flow expectations. Utilities fell 2.91%, Materials 1.86% and Industrials 1.83%; higher long yields and repeated capex-duration shocks left rate-sensitive and cyclical breadth behind the headline indices.

The week's key causal split was demonstrated monetization versus capital intensity. Microsoft rose 15.51% on July 30 as public reporting tied its result to stronger profit and AI-linked demand; the move lifted LRCX 17.98%, MRVL 12.18%, INTC 11.30% and ANET 8.26%. Meta fell 7.95% the same day after raising its AI-investment forecast, and Apple fell 7.35% the next day. Amazon's 15.32% gain then showed that the market will finance large AI budgets when the forward revenue and margin bridge appears credible.

Cross-asset evidence improved by Friday but did not remove the rate constraint. VIX ended at 15.99, credit remained firmer than intermediate Treasuries, and 69.0% of the repaired industry universe was above the 200-day average. Yet the 10-year yield reached 4.745%, IWM lagged, and long Treasuries fell on the final session. The result is a constructive but selective regime: earnings proof can overcome duration pressure for individual winners, while weaker balance sheets and unproven capex stories remain vulnerable.

Portfolio implication: preserve breadth, but concentrate active risk in businesses where estimate revisions can validate AI or energy cash-flow conversion. Avoid extrapolating one-day semiconductor rebounds when five-day momentum remains damaged, and do not interpret SPY strength as full participation while IWM and several cyclicals lag. Confirmation would be stable credit, a lower 10-year yield, improving small-cap relative strength and sustained 50-day breadth; falsification would be another simultaneous equity-bond selloff, widening HYG/IEF, VIX re-expansion and 200-day breadth below 50%.
