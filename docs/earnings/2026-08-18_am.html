Home Depot (HD) — Tier 1

Subsector mechanics and business delta

Home Depot is not simply a housing-starts trade. Its near-term earnings algorithm is a interaction among repair-and-remodel demand, transaction count, average ticket, Pro customer activity, product inflation, gross-margin discipline and fixed-cost leverage across a very large store and distribution base. The most valuable signal is therefore not nominal sales alone: it is whether customer projects broaden enough to turn transactions positive without sacrificing price, mix or promotional discipline. The SRS/GMS acquisitions add a second engine tied to roofing, pools and specialty trade distribution, but they also add leverage, amortization and integration complexity.

The quarter moved the business modestly forward, not decisively out of the housing-repair trough. Sales rose 5.7% to $47.861 billion and comparable sales rose 1.7%, with US comps at 1.3%. Average ticket increased 2.8% to $92.50, but transactions declined 1.0%. That composition says the top line still depends on price/mix and larger professional projects rather than broad traffic. GAAP operating margin compressed to 14.3% from 14.5%; adjusted operating margin eased to 14.7% from 14.8%. First-half operating cash flow rose to $11.422 billion from $8.968 billion while capital expenditure was essentially unchanged at $1.724 billion. Cash conversion is the best buried positive; negative transactions remain the principal causal weak point.

Expectations, variance and quality

Public FactSet consensus reported by AP was $47.24 billion of revenue and $4.73 of adjusted EPS. Actual revenue beat by roughly $621 million, or 1.3%, and adjusted EPS of $4.92 beat by $0.19, or 4.0%. The company did not raise its fiscal-year framework: it reaffirmed sales growth of 2.5%-4.5%, comps of flat to +2%, adjusted operating margin of 12.8%-13.0%, interest expense near $2.3 billion and adjusted EPS from flat to +4% versus $14.69. The stock's positive premarket reaction reflects relief that demand and margins did not deteriorate, rather than a higher official earnings ceiling.

The mechanical EPS-quality gate is cautious. GAAP EPS was $4.79 and the acquisition-related amortization adjustment was $0.13 per share. That adjustment is about 68% of the $0.19 adjusted-EPS beat versus consensus, above the 30% low-quality threshold. It does not mean the underlying quarter was poor: positive comps, better cash flow and an operating margin close to last year corroborate real operating resilience. It does mean the headline adjusted-EPS surprise should not be capitalized at a premium without transaction recovery. The rate-of-change map is mixed: sales and cash conversion accelerated, transactions remained negative, and adjusted operating margin slipped ten basis points. Timing effects and acquisition accounting explain part, but not all, of the variance.

KPI tree, model and valuation lattice

The three causal KPIs are US comps, transaction growth and adjusted operating margin. US comps determine organic store productivity; transactions show whether demand is broadening beyond price/mix; margin determines how much revenue converts to earnings. Secondary watch items are Pro versus DIY growth, big-ticket project demand, inventory turns, gross margin, SRS/GMS integration, interest expense and capital expenditure. A compound warning would be transactions below zero plus US comps below zero plus adjusted operating margin below 12.8%: that combination would imply demand and leverage are deteriorating together.

Using the prior-year sales base embedded in management's framework, FY26 sales are approximately $168.8-$172.1 billion and adjusted EPS $14.69-$15.28. A reasonable FY27 band is 2%-5% sales growth and roughly $15.00-$16.25 of EPS, contingent on transaction recovery and stable margin. At the $344.35 premarket price, the shares trade near 22.5-23.4 times the FY26 EPS range. The valuation lattice is $294 in a bear case at 20 times $14.70, $352 in a base case at 23 times $15.30, and $400 in a bull case at 25 times $16.00. The base case leaves limited immediate upside after the relief move; the bull case needs traffic and Pro projects to broaden.

Debate, action and call agenda

Bull claim: resilient positive comps, strong cash generation and an intact FY range show that housing-repair demand is bottoming; evidence is the 1.3% US comp and 27% first-half operating-cash-flow increase. Bear claim: a negative transaction count and slight margin erosion show no clean volume-led inflection; the $0.13 adjustment also supplies most of the EPS surprise. Variant claim: the market may underweight SRS/GMS cash and Pro synergies but is already paying a normal-to-rich multiple before transaction recovery. The falsifier for the bull case is another negative comp or adjusted margin below 12.8%; the falsifier for the bear case is positive transactions with US comps above 2% and margin at or above 12.9%.

The action is HOLD / WAIT, not chase the premarket move. Upgrade only when transaction growth reaches at least zero, US comps sustain above 2%, and adjusted operating margin remains at least 12.9%. The three required call questions are: (1) what proportion of the comp improvement came from Pro, big-ticket projects, inflation and acquired sales, and when should transactions turn positive; (2) what is the gross-margin and sourcing bridge for tariffs and IEEPA-related costs across the second half; and (3) how are GMS/SRS integration milestones and operating cadence being governed during the CEO's medical leave? Baidu (BIDU) — Tier 1

Subsector mechanics and business delta

Baidu is in a difficult search-to-AI transition. The economic question is whether GPU cloud, model services and AI-native applications can grow fast enough—and at sufficient incremental margin—to replace a shrinking online-marketing pool before legacy cash generation erodes. Revenue quality depends on AI infrastructure utilization, accelerator supply, pricing and depreciation; search monetization depends on query volume, ad load and advertiser ROI. Apollo adds long-duration autonomy optionality, but the near-term earnings bridge remains advertising decline versus AI Cloud scale.

Second-quarter revenue was RMB31.3 billion, down 4% year over year and 2% sequentially. Baidu General revenue was RMB25.2 billion, down 4%, while online marketing fell 19% to RMB13.1 billion. Other Baidu General revenue grew 21% to RMB12.1 billion. Management identified RMB12.5 billion of AI-powered business revenue, around half of Baidu General. AI Cloud infrastructure revenue rose 50% to RMB7.3 billion, with GPU Cloud up 283%; AI applications grew only 3% to RMB2.5 billion and AI-native marketing was flat at RMB2.6 billion. iQIYI revenue fell 5% to RMB6.3 billion. The transition is real but incomplete: fast infrastructure growth is not yet offsetting the size and profitability of search advertising.

Expectations, variance and quality

The release provided no explicit quarterly revenue guide. Public consensus compiled by ChartMill was RMB33.133 billion of revenue and RMB12.11 of EPS per ADS. Actual revenue missed by RMB1.833 billion, or 5.5%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of RMB7.22 missed by RMB4.89, or roughly 40%. Operating income was RMB3.0 billion at a 10% margin; non-GAAP operating income was RMB3.8 billion at 12%. Non-GAAP net income was RMB2.6 billion. Adjusted EBITDA was RMB6.2 billion at a 20% margin, while operating cash flow was just RMB3.4 billion. Cash, restricted cash and short-term investments of RMB283.1 billion preserve strategic capacity, but cash generation is well below the balance-sheet headline.

This is a negative operating variance, not a cosmetic accounting miss. The first layer is mix: advertising contracted much faster than AI infrastructure grew in absolute profit contribution. The second is margin and cash conversion: GPU infrastructure can require heavy capital and depreciation before utilization matures. The rate of change is bifurcated—GPU Cloud accelerated from an already rapid base, but marketing fell almost one-fifth and AI applications barely grew. No identified adjustment explains 30% or more of the EPS miss, so the EPS-quality gate does not excuse the result. The critical structural-versus-timing question is whether advertiser disruption is temporary during product migration or a durable loss of search economics.

KPI tree, model and valuation lattice

The causal KPIs are AI Cloud growth, online-marketing growth and non-GAAP operating margin. Cloud growth measures replacement-revenue velocity; marketing decline measures the size of the hole; operating margin determines whether AI scale produces economic value. Operating cash flow, GPU utilization, capex/depreciation, AI application growth and Apollo paid rides are supporting measures. A compound deterioration is AI Cloud below 30%, marketing near -20% and non-GAAP operating margin below 10%—that would show that both transition speed and legacy funding are failing.

A provisional FY26 range is RMB126-RMB132 billion of revenue and RMB38-RMB44 of non-GAAP EPS per ADS. FY27 could reach RMB134-RMB145 billion and RMB45-RMB58 per ADS only if cloud remains above 35%-40%, search decline moderates and utilization supports margin. Using the release's approximate RMB6.78 per US dollar translation, FY26 EPS is about $5.60-$6.50; the $96 premarket ADR therefore trades around 15-17 times that range. A bear case of $70 applies 13 times $5.40; a base of $105 applies 17 times $6.20; a bull of $140 applies 20 times $7.00. The cash balance supports the downside, but it should not be valued dollar-for-dollar without proof that reinvestment earns adequate returns.

Debate, action and call agenda

Bull claim: 50% AI Cloud infrastructure growth and 283% GPU Cloud growth demonstrate platform relevance, while the cash balance funds the transition. Bear claim: a 19% advertising decline, 5.5% revenue miss, 40% EPS miss and weak cash conversion show that AI revenue remains economically insufficient. Variant claim: the market may focus on AI growth rates without separating capital-intensive infrastructure revenue from higher-quality application and advertising economics. The bull case is falsified if AI Cloud drops below 30% or cash generation stays below RMB4 billion; the bear case is falsified if marketing improves above -10%, cloud remains above 40% and margins stabilize.

The decision is WAIT after the 7.8% premarket decline. A lower price alone does not resolve the transition. Upgrade when AI growth offsets the absolute marketing decline, operating cash flow recovers above RMB4 billion and non-GAAP operating margin stays above 12%. The three call questions are: (1) what GPU capacity, utilization, depreciation and gross-margin assumptions underpin the cloud outlook; (2) in what quarter should AI Cloud and applications fully offset the decline in online marketing; and (3) what advertiser-cohort, query-monetization and Apollo unit-economic evidence supports stabilization? Amer Sports (AS) — Tier 1

Subsector mechanics and business delta

Amer Sports is a portfolio of premium technical brands whose economics are driven by full-price demand, direct-to-consumer penetration, product scarcity, geographic mix and inventory discipline. Arc'teryx supplies high-margin technical apparel; Salomon supplies footwear and outdoor equipment; Wilson supplies ball and racquet sports. The essential analytical distinction is between authentic brand heat that supports full-price sell-through and temporary accounting or tariff benefits. Growth can destroy value if inventory, wholesale discounting or store costs outrun demand.

Second-quarter revenue rose 32% to $1.633 billion, or 30% in constant currency. Technical Apparel revenue increased 32% to $674.2 million and Arc'teryx omni-channel comps were 17%. Outdoor Performance grew 37% to $568.5 million, and Ball & Racquet Sports grew 24% to $389.9 million. Reported adjusted gross margin reached 65.8% and adjusted operating margin 12.8%. Adjusted EPS was $0.22 and adjusted net income $127 million. Inventory increased 19% to $1.897 billion, slower than reported sales but still a material absolute commitment. Net cash was $573 million. Breadth across all three segments is the most important positive; inventory conversion remains the important second-half proof point.

Expectations, variance and quality

Management's prior Q2 framework called for 22%-24% revenue growth, approximately 59.5% gross margin, 6%-7% operating margin and $0.08-$0.10 of EPS. Public consensus reported by Benzinga was roughly $1.54 billion of revenue and $0.10 of EPS. Actual revenue exceeded consensus by about $93 million, or 6%, and adjusted EPS exceeded it by $0.12. Management raised FY26 revenue growth to approximately 24%, gross margin to 60.5%-61.0%, operating margin to 14.2%-14.5% and EPS to $1.27-$1.30 from $1.18-$1.23. Q3 guidance calls for 18%-20% revenue growth, roughly 59% gross margin, 13.5%-14.0% operating margin and $0.31-$0.33 of EPS.

The headline beat has a substantial non-recurring component. Tariff refunds added $50.1 million, approximately 390 basis points to gross and operating margin. Removing that benefit puts underlying adjusted gross margin near 61.9% and adjusted operating margin near 8.9%. At an illustrative 28% tax rate and the current diluted share base, the refund contributed roughly $0.06 per share, around half of the $0.12 EPS surprise. That exceeds the 30% low-quality threshold. Yet the quarter is not merely low quality: revenue beat prior guidance materially, every segment grew above 20%, and Arc'teryx comps remained strong. The correct interpretation is a high-quality revenue beat paired with a partly temporary margin/EPS beat.

KPI tree, model and valuation lattice

The causal KPIs are Arc'teryx omni-channel comps, Outdoor Performance growth and underlying gross margin excluding tariff refunds. They measure brand heat, portfolio breadth and true product economics. Inventory growth versus sales, wholesale order quality, DTC mix, China growth, full-price sell-through and promotional cadence are supporting measures. A compound warning is Arc'teryx comps below 10%, inventory growth above 25%, and underlying gross margin below 60%; that would imply brand normalization and excess product at the same time.

The raised FY26 EPS range is $1.27-$1.30. A provisional FY27 range is $1.50-$1.70 if revenue remains in the mid-to-high teens and operating leverage persists without the tariff refund. At $34.04, the shares trade around 26.2-26.8 times FY26 EPS and 20-23 times the FY27 range. A bear value of $26 applies 20 times $1.30; a base of $35 applies 23 times $1.52; a bull of $44 applies 26 times $1.70. The premarket move places the stock close to the base case, leaving execution rather than multiple expansion as the required return driver.

Debate, action and call agenda

Bull claim: segment breadth, 17% Arc'teryx comps and a raised full-year framework demonstrate durable premium-brand demand. Bear claim: about half the EPS surprise came from a tariff refund, while inventory remains nearly $1.9 billion and growth decelerates in Q3. Variant claim: the market may either over-credit the refund or under-credit the underlying revenue beat; the durable signal is full-price sell-through and inventory conversion, not reported margin alone. The bull case is falsified by Arc'teryx comps below 10% or inventory growth above 25%; the bear case is falsified by underlying gross margin above 61%, inventory growth below sales and Outdoor Performance growth above 25%.

The action is HOLD / WAIT after the 4.5% premarket rise. Add only after the company proves that raised guidance survives normalization of tariff benefits. The three call questions are: (1) provide the exact tariff-refund bridge, timing and recurrence embedded in Q3 and FY gross margin; (2) distinguish DTC, wholesale, China and product-category drivers for Arc'teryx and Salomon; and (3) what inventory, receipts and full-price sell-through assumptions are required to convert the Q3 guide? The call-node deadline is 2026-08-18 20:00 America/Toronto.

Klarna (KLAR) — Tier 1

Subsector mechanics and business delta

Klarna is a two-sided payments and credit platform. GMV is the activity base; revenue yield depends on merchant fees, consumer products, card economics and fair-value accounting; transaction margin dollars subtract funding, credit and processing costs. The quality of growth therefore depends on transaction margin as a percentage of GMV, provision losses, funding mix and repeat consumer engagement—not GMV alone. Consumer deposits funding roughly 90% of the balance sheet reduce wholesale-funding risk, while credit underwriting and regulatory capital remain key failure modes.

Q2 GMV reached $36.6 billion, up 18%; revenue increased 27% to $1.042 billion; transaction margin dollars rose 42% to $446 million; and adjusted operating income rose 214% to $91 million. GAAP operating income was $27 million versus a $46 million loss, and net income was $9 million versus a $53 million loss. Provision losses improved to 0.52% of GMV from 0.56%. Active consumers reached 120 million, up 8%, while annualized revenue per active consumer grew 24%. Memberships reached 2 million and active card users 6.5 million. Transaction margin dollars were 42.8% of revenue, up 4.5 points. These are genuine unit-economic improvements.

Expectations, variance and quality

The prior Q2 guide was $35.5-$36.5 billion of GMV, $960 million-$1.0 billion of revenue, $375-$395 million of transaction margin dollars and $30-$50 million of adjusted operating income. Actuals exceeded the top end by $0.1 billion, $42 million, $51 million and $41 million, respectively. Public consensus reported by Investing.com was approximately $992.8 million of revenue and a $0.05 adjusted loss per share; actual revenue beat by about 5% and adjusted EPS was $0.01.

The stock fell because the forward bridge deteriorated. Full-year GMV is now $149-$151 billion, below the prior greater-than-$155 billion objective, partly from currency and German volume weakness. Revenue is guided to $4.08-$4.16 billion versus public consensus near $4.42 billion. Management raised transaction margin dollars to $1.62-$1.65 billion and guided adjusted operating income to $280-$300 million, but Q3 adjusted operating income is only $5-$15 million on $940-$980 million of revenue as investment rises. A fair-value accounting change reduces reported revenue by roughly ten basis points of GMV with matching cost effects and adds about two basis points to transaction margin timing. That is presentation and timing, not cash economics; the structural questions are German demand and investment returns.

No disclosed one-time EPS adjustment accounts for 30% or more of the operating beat, so the mechanical quality gate is positive. The tension is instead between strong current unit economics and weaker volume/revenue expectations. Rate of change is positive in transaction margin, credit losses, card adoption and profitability, but negative in full-year GMV and the immediate Q3 earnings cadence. Planned CFO Niclas Neglén and CMO David Sandström transitions effective in early 2027 add a governance and continuity item while the company scales.

KPI tree, model and valuation lattice

The causal KPIs are transaction margin dollars as a percentage of GMV, provision losses as a percentage of GMV and US/German GMV growth. They capture unit economics, credit quality and geographic demand. Funding cost, consumer deposits, merchant yield, card usage, annualized revenue per consumer and operating expense are supporting measures. A compound warning is provision losses above 0.70%, transaction margin below 1.05% of GMV and GMV below $149 billion; that would invalidate the scale-and-efficiency thesis.

FY26 revenue is guided to $4.08-$4.16 billion and adjusted operating income to $280-$300 million. GAAP EPS and free-cash-flow guidance are not available from the release, so substituting an invented EPS bridge would be misleading. A provisional FY27 range is $4.6-$5.0 billion of revenue and $420-$550 million of adjusted operating income if German volume stabilizes and US margins converge upward. At the $16.15 premarket price and roughly $7.37 billion market capitalization, the stock is near 1.8 times FY26 revenue and about 25 times adjusted operating income before tax, interest and stock compensation. A bear equity value of $5.5 billion assumes 1.3 times $4.2 billion; a base of $8.5 billion assumes 1.8 times $4.7 billion; a bull of $12 billion assumes 2.4 times $5.0 billion.

Debate, action and call agenda

Bull claim: transaction margin dollars grew 42%, provision losses improved and GAAP profitability emerged, demonstrating better unit economics. Bear claim: full-year volume and revenue were reset below expectations, Q3 profitability nearly disappears and Germany is weak. Variant claim: the 17% price drop may conflate non-cash accounting presentation with real demand weakness; only the latter deserves a durable discount. The bull case is falsified if transaction margin falls below 1.05% of GMV or provisions exceed 0.70%; the bear case is falsified if German growth stabilizes, US transaction margin rises above 27% and full-year transaction margin exceeds $1.65 billion.

The decision is WAIT. Do not average down merely because the multiple compressed. Upgrade after the volume reset stabilizes and Q3 investment produces measurable consumer, merchant or margin returns. The three call questions are: (1) quantify German GMV weakness by cohort and identify the path to stabilization; (2) reconcile fair-value-accounting changes to cash revenue, transaction margin and full-year comparisons; and (3) explain the CFO/CMO succession timeline, responsibilities and control continuity during a high-growth investment year.
