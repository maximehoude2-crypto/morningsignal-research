EPS-quality gate: GAAP EPS was $3.17 versus adjusted EPS of $3.50. The $0.33 bridge included a $0.31 unrealized strategic-investment loss exclusion, acquisition items and tax adjustments. That exclusion is large relative to the $0.11 adjusted-EPS beat, but consensus was itself adjusted; there is no evidence that a one-time benefit generated more than 30% of the like-for-like adjusted beat. The underlying revenue and operating-margin evidence therefore supports the quality of the adjusted beat, while the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge remains material and explicit.

Operating-engine diagnosis

  1. Semiconductor Systems revenue and mix. Systems revenue rose to $7.040 billion from $5.564 billion a year ago and $5.965 billion in Q2. DRAM represented 26% of Systems versus 22% a year ago; foundry/logic was 67% versus 69%; Flash was 7% versus 9%. The mechanism is favorable: HBM and leading-edge logic require more deposition, etch, CMP, metrology and advanced-packaging process steps per wafer. More process complexity increases Applied's content opportunity even before unit wafer starts rise.

  2. Systems margin conversion. Systems gross margin reached 55.3% and operating margin 37.7%, versus 53.4% and 33.0% a year ago. Revenue grew 26.5%, but operating income grew 44.6% to $2.657 billion. This is the operating leverage the valuation needs: mix and scale converted incremental revenue into profit faster than sales.

  3. Applied Global Services annuity. AGS revenue rose 21.7% to $1.781 billion, gross margin expanded 180 bps to 35.6%, and operating margin expanded 280 bps to 30.1%. A larger installed base and higher tool utilization feed spares, service, upgrades and optimization revenue. AGS growth reduces the cyclicality of the consolidated model and supports a higher earnings floor.

  4. Cash conversion and capacity. Quarterly operating cash flow rebounded to $3.037 billion and free cash flow to $2.330 billion after only $210 million of non-GAAP FCF in Q2. That reversal is constructive. However, nine-month working-capital use was $1.877 billion; accounts receivable rose to $7.691 billion from $5.185 billion at fiscal year-end, faster than the revenue run rate, while inventory rose to $6.564 billion from $5.915 billion. The buried signal is two-sided: contract liabilities rose to $3.271 billion from $2.566 billion, supporting customer commitments, but receivables and inventory show that fulfilling the AI ramp is becoming more balance-sheet intensive. Q4 cash conversion must prove this is growth investment rather than slippage.

Compound flag: Revenue acceleration, margin expansion, AGS growth and higher contract liabilities are causally connected positives: AI-driven equipment demand expands both current tool revenue and the future service base. The countervailing receivables/inventory build is also causally linked to the ramp. The net compound remains positive only if Q4 converts the working capital to cash.
