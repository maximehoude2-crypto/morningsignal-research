[[MSFT]] — Microsoft

Decision line. Microsoft reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $90.007B, up 18%, operating income of $40.603B, up 18%, GAAP EPS of $4.81, and non-GAAP EPS of $4.74. The EPS beat does not fully pass the 30% quality gate. Management disclosed roughly $0.27 per share of benefit from a $3.2B Anthropic investment gain and lower employee-related value-repurchase costs, partly offset by severance and an Xbox impairment. Against a $0.53 beat versus the dated $4.21 point estimate, that benefit explains about 51%. The right conclusion is not that the quarter was weak; it is that Azure, cloud revenue, and RPO—not the headline EPS variance—carry the thesis.

The causal chain is unusually clean. Microsoft added 31 data centers and roughly one gigawatt of capacity, reduced dock-to-live timing, and used fleet efficiency to turn constrained supply into billable Azure compute. That capacity supported 43% Azure growth, while contracted cloud demand lifted RPO. The installed enterprise base then distributes Copilot: paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats exceeded 30M, up from more than 20M last quarter, and deployment-to-high-usage time has fallen from months to days. The economic flywheel is capacity → consumption → enterprise workflow penetration → seat and usage monetization → backlog and cash flow. The constraint is capex intensity and power: fiscal Q1 capex above $50B means the denominator for return on invested capital rises faster than reported depreciation.

Nadella cited 30M paid seats, much faster deployment, usage patterns approaching Outlook/Teams, and both per-seat and consumption monetization; Hood called E7 early rather than presenting it as booked revenue.

[[ARM]] — Arm Holdings

Decision line. Arm reported fiscal Q1 revenue of $1.29B, up 22%, royalty revenue of $715M, up 22%, license revenue of $574M, up 23%, non-GAAP EPS of $0.45, and non-GAAP operating margin of 41%. The dated Nasdaq calendar showed an anomalous $0.18 EPS point estimate that conflicts with other public expectations and is not used as the hurdle. The valuation-implied hurdle is far higher: Arm must sustain 20%+ royalties, convert AGI CPU demand into several billion dollars of revenue, and protect margins while mobile weakens. Action: WAIT / AVOID NEW MONEY; medium-high conviction.

Cloud and data-center royalties more than doubled, the installed base reached roughly 1.5B Neoverse cores, and management now sees more than $2B of AGI CPU demand, up from the prior $1B opportunity. Supply is secured for the initial $1B and management is more confident in delivering above that level. Those are real positives. The quality caveats are equally concrete. A SoftBank-related arrangement contributed $193M of license revenue and should contribute roughly $200M per quarter for the rest of the year. The transaction may be economically valid, but a related-party customer changes the quality of organic license growth. Smartphone unit demand is down double digits and weakness has spread into upper and mid tiers. Armv9/CSS raises content, but management reduced its overall royalty-growth expectation to high teens from roughly 20%.

First-generation AGI CPU gross margin is expected in the high-30s/low-40s before improving toward 50% over a couple of years. That is materially below the economics of pure IP licensing. The strategic opportunity expands Arm's addressable market, but it also changes the business mix toward inventory, substrates, wafers, memory, and execution risk. The company believes long-run “intelligence” economics will justify it; investors must not capitalize mature IP margins onto an immature silicon product.

Q&A, prior change, and scenario bridge

Analysts pressed on whether demand or supply caps AGI revenue. Management said demand exceeds $2B while near-term deliverable supply is above $1B, with wafers, substrates, testing, and memory all relevant. On gross margin, management gave the high-30s/low-40s starting point and a roughly two-year path toward 50%, while acknowledging component inflation may require price adjustments. On smartphones, management admitted the slowdown is broadening and guided next-quarter royalty growth to low/mid-teens. On the outer opportunity, management cited external market estimates above $200B but refused to formally raise its own $100B framework, focusing on next year's $2B opportunity.

Versus May, the AGI narrative moved from a $1B opportunity with doubled demand to more than $2B demand and better supply confidence. At the same time, the current call introduced a lower-margin starting point and broader mobile weakness. Prior optimism therefore converted into a more measurable but less pristine P&L bridge. The explicit omission is the customer concentration and non-cancellable commitment structure behind AGI demand, along with the economics of the SoftBank-related license transaction. Sentiment is +50 versus +75, a -25 sharp deterioration in visibility despite high enthusiasm. Answer quality is 72/100 and pressure delta -50.

FY1/FY2 scenarios. Q2 guidance is $1.38B ±$50M revenue and $0.47 ±$0.04 non-GAAP EPS, with license growth around 30% and royalty growth in the low teens. Base: annual revenue reaches $6.0B, AGI supply converts but dilutes margin, and EPS reaches $2.10; a 70x multiple gives $147. Bull: $7.0B revenue and $2.75 EPS as AGI supply and cloud royalties accelerate; 80x gives $220. Bear: mobile weakness and supply limits hold revenue to $5.2B and EPS to $1.60; 45x gives $72. Weighted 50/25/25, value is roughly $147. The large valuation gap is why a 6% after-hours decline does not create a sufficient margin of safety.
